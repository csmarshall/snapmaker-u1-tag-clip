Shorter feet and the grip band

How the pieces go together

Chip
Clips onto the spool's filament end on the shelf; drops into the dock from above when you load that spool.
Dock
One print for every mount. Its flat back goes against the panel over the antenna, taped there or held by the dock mount.
Dock mount
Fits either adapter. Its tongue, the shape of the slot less 0.3 mm all round, slides 30 mm down into the slot from above, and a crossbar rests on the adapter's top edge. The dock stands on the crossbar, and two arms rise beside it to L clamps over its middle band: a side wall, a front jaw over the rail's edge and a tongue in the side groove, with the panel closing the C. The dock pushes in from the front (Explode shows the motion); lift the mount out to clear the socket. It never touches the dock's back, so the sticker is as close to the antenna as when taped.
Socket adapter
Ours, in this repo. remmulegguh's snap fingers go into the socket behind a 30.5 × 43 × 7.87 mm plate with 1waynger1's slot copied from measurements. Its top edge is level with the fingers' top, 5 mm above the socket axis, so the sticker sits 28.6 mm above the axis.
Hub adapter
1waynger1's base (MakerWorld 2152529), if you already run it: lift his spool arm out and drop the dock mount in. The sticker sits 43.0 mm above the axis with the base's plug-near end up, 47.2 mm the other way (HUB_NEAR_END_UP, not yet checked on your printer). The beige base in the viewer is a stand-in drawn from my measurements, not his file.
Blank cover
The same snap fingers with a plain round plate and the slot number, for a socket you aren't using.

Printing the socket adapter and dock mount

How to print it

Chip
Back face down, no pause. Press the sticker into the 0.6 mm recess in its back afterwards. The chip is 2.4 mm thick.
Dock mount, socket adapter
Each standing on its top end; see the figure above.
Dock
Standing on its bottom stop (flat end down), so the cover, lips and funnel are all vertical or slope upward.
Two colours
Body plus label STL for each chip, and dock plus digit STL for each slot, sharing one origin.

Review findings and what changed

Open

Read not yet tested on a printer

Snapmaker's photos show each reader coil as a loop about 126 mm across (an estimate, 110 to 143) around its socket, and the advice online is to keep the tag within 1 to 3 cm. The sticker now faces the panel from an open recess in the chip's back, behind only the dock's 1.2 mm back plate, and sits 28.6 mm above the axis on our socket adapter, well inside the loop.

Next: print the one-plate test set (u1/docs/TEST_PRINT.md); the first docked read is the test. If it fails, the coil-finder puck and standoff strip in u1/fixtures/ find out why, and the back plate (DOCK_FLOOR) can get thinner.

Fixed

The top rim is one flat plane

The notches came from separate wedge cuts at different depths. The mouth is now one ruled loft from the chip's path up to a flared opening, cut from every part at once, so the back plate, channels and lips all end on the same flat rim with an even bevel inside. A test checks that every point near the top sits at one height.

Fixed

Printable without bridges

The sticker recess opens on the bed side, and its ceiling bridges about 26mm, so the chip needs no supports. The dock prints upright. The lips have 45° lower ends, and the lower pocket is closed at the bottom by a full-depth bottom stop.

Fixed

Upside-down chips can't seat

The flat end is 4 mm longer with a centre notch (the ghost feet), and a 3 mm key post stands on the bottom stop. Put in round end first, the chip lands on the post and sits about 3 mm high; a test sweeps a flipped chip down to confirm it.

Fixed

One clearance, named parts, checks in the repo

Every sliding gap derives from FIT_CLEARANCE (0.3 mm; 0.6 mm over the clip arms). The dock is built as back plate, channels and lower pocket, and these are exported as separate files, which this viewer loads directly. The side-slot code is gone, since the clip is empty when docked. Thirty-one checks are now in tests/test_fit.py, including the comparison with Extrutim's clip.

Open

Smaller items

Label sizes still vary by material (auto-fit). The thin 0.4 mm lip edge at the rim may print ragged. Sticker thickness is unmeasured, and the 0.6 mm recess assumes about 0.4 mm. Two-colour import into Snapmaker Orca hasn't been tried.

What the checks do and don't show

Checks that use someone else's file, so they are independent of this model: with 1waynger1's real base placed in the dock's frame (turned 180° about X, a proper rotation), its screw hole lands on the socket axis the model assumes; the dock mount lowered from 35 mm above slides into his slot without touching it, and once seated cannot drop or move 0.5 mm sideways or away from the panel; and over the middle of the plate, our socket adapter and our stand-in each match his slotted plate within 2% by volume, so our copy of his slot is his slot. The socket adapter's fingers match remmulegguh's within 2%. They do not show which end of his base is up on your printer, or how tight the tongue feels in print.

Passing: watertight parts that add up to the dock; no chip overlaps its dock; a chip lowered from 30 mm seats without touching; chips 1.8 mm off-centre, 0.4 mm forward or 0.6 mm back drop into the funnel; a flipped chip stops on the key post; the back plate covers the whole sticker; the sticker recess is open at the back and deeper than the sticker; labels stay inside the edge; the hook finger stands at least 2 mm proud of the lower pocket. With the dock mount in either adapter, the chip still slides in from above without touching anything; the dock seats in the mount without touching it; pushing the dock in from the front meets only the jaws and tongues, only in the band; and a seated dock moved 1 mm up, down or away from the panel runs into the clamps. The clip matches Extrutim's original within 0.1 mm wherever it was sampled. All but that last check use numbers from the same script that builds the parts. They show the files agree with themselves, not that anything fits a printer or a real sticker.
